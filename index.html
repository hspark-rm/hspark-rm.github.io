I study how social disadvantage accumulates over the life course, and how it shapes well-being among immigrants, youth, and workers in South Korea.

My work is grounded in medical sociology and uses longitudinal panel methods — fixed effects, asymmetric fixed effects, and sibling fixed effects — to identify within-person and within-family change rather than cross-sectional association.

About

I received my Ph.D. in Public Health Sciences from Korea University in 2025, advised by Jinho Kim. My dissertation, Discrimination and Well-Being Across Generations, followed immigrant women and multicultural adolescents in South Korea over time, asking how experiences of discrimination move across generations within families.

I am currently a research professor at the Institute on Aging, Korea University, where I extend this life course work into later life. Previously I was a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Public Administration at Ewha Womans University. Before my doctorate I completed an M.A. in Development Policy at the KDI School of Public Policy and Management, and worked as a policy researcher.

My research has appeared in Social Science & Medicine, Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry, Population Research and Policy Review, and Child Abuse & Neglect, among others.

Research interests

Discrimination and immigrant well-being · Precarious employment and the labour market · Adolescent mental health · Population aging and later life · Life course and intergenerational transmission · Longitudinal and panel data methods
